Random Tick-Tack-Toe

This is Assignment 2 for the SM5350 (Game Design Fundamentals) course!

Assignment Requirements: Add a few rules to the original Tic-Tac-Toe game to make it more interesting.

Game Rules

  • Two players use O and X pieces, respectively, and take turns placing pieces on a 6 x 6 square board.
  • Before placing a piece, each player must roll a die (with values from 1 to 6). Let the rolled number be i; the player may only place a piece on the i-th row or the i-th column of the board.
  • Each player has one chance to re-roll the die per game.
  • If a player rolls an i and every position in the i-th row and i-th column is already occupied by a piece, that player may re-roll the die once for free.
  • Victory Condition: A player wins when four of their pieces form a straight line on the board (horizontal, vertical, or diagonal). If the board is full and no one has achieved a line of four, the game automatically ends in a draw.

    Design Concept

    Incorporating Randomness

    The game state in the original Tic-Tac-Toe is too predictable. By adding randomness, the course of each game becomes unique, greatly increasing players’ desire to play repeatedly. This follows the same logic as Roguelike games, capitalizing on the player’s mindset that “I’m sure I’ll win next time.”

    Furthermore, when playing with friends, randomness brings unexpected surprises to the gameplay (such as rolling a string of terrible numbers), which greatly enhances the game’s enjoyment. This is something I discovered during playtests with friends—after all, when it comes to games, neither absolute balance nor strategic depth is as important as ensuring players have fun!

    The Dialectical Unity of Constraints and Freedom

    On the surface, the dice roll mechanism limits players’ choices, but it also grants them freedom in making specific decisions. This echoes a perspective we explored in the SM5350 course: constraints in games aren’t obstacles; rather, appropriate rules can better stimulate players’ tactical thinking and creativity.
